# LinkedIn Content Strategy — Private Health Insurance Germany
**Audience:** Software engineers, IT professionals, expats relocating to Germany  
**Language:** English  
**Goal:** Build a following that eventually converts to insurance inquiries  
**Posting cadence:** 3-4x per week

---

## The Core Problem
Health insurance is boring. But money, career moves, bureaucracy nightmares, and "things they don't tell you about Germany" are not.

Health insurance is the *angle* — not the *topic*.

---

## Content Mix (The 70/20/10 Rule)

### 70% — VIRAL HOOKS (broad appeal, massive reach)
These posts are not about health insurance. They're about things engineers and expats care about deeply. Health insurance appears as a subplot or punchline — not the headline.

**Formats that work for this audience:**
- `I moved to Germany earning €X. Here's what surprised me.`
- `The German bureaucracy thing nobody tells you about`
- `6 months in Berlin as a software engineer. My honest take.`
- `Things I wish I knew before moving to Germany` (list format)
- `Germany vs [US/UK/Netherlands] for software engineers` (comparison)
- `The hidden cost of working in Germany` (net vs gross salary content)
- `Why I almost left Germany — and why I stayed`

**Why it works:** These posts get shared by expat communities, tech Slack channels, and relocation groups. They bring followers who stick around because you're genuinely useful.

---

### 20% — EDUCATION POSTS (build authority, medium reach)
These are the "smart insider" posts. Teach something non-obvious about the German healthcare/insurance system. No selling. Just clarity.

**Formats:**
- `Most people don't know X about German health insurance`
- `The €X threshold that changes everything` (PKV eligibility)
- `GKV vs PKV: what the comparison sites don't tell you`
- `Why your German health insurance premium can go down (if you're smart about it)`
- `The retirement trap inside German public health insurance`

**Why it works:** Engineers trust data. Give them the math, not the marketing. These posts establish you as the expert without feeling like an ad.

---

### 10% — DIRECT VALUE (soft conversion)
These are the closest thing to a sales post — but framed as genuine help. Only works if the audience already trusts you from the 70% and 20% content.

**Formats:**
- `How to switch from GKV to PKV (step-by-step)`
- `I compared 5 private health insurers in Germany so you don't have to`
- `The checklist I wish I had when choosing health insurance in Germany`
- `DM me if you want the spreadsheet I used to compare PKV premiums`

**Why it works:** By this point, the reader already follows you. They ask for it.

---

## Viral Mechanics for This Audience

| Hook Type | Why It Works for Engineers |
|---|---|
| Numbers / data | Engineers trust math over marketing |
| "Things nobody told me" | Triggers information asymmetry curiosity |
| Germany vs. X comparisons | Expats love validating their move decision |
| Bureaucracy horror stories | Universal expat experience — instant relatability |
| Career + money framing | Health insurance as a financial decision, not a form to fill |
| "I made this mistake" | Vulnerability + lesson = highest comment rate |

---

## Platform Mechanics to Exploit

- **Hook line must stand alone** — LinkedIn truncates after line 1 on mobile
- **No links in the post body** — LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound links; put URL in first comment
- **Ask a question at the end** — drives comments, which boost reach dramatically
- **Reply to every comment in the first hour** — LinkedIn's algorithm rewards engagement velocity
- **Post Tuesday–Thursday, 7–9am CET** — when European tech workers scroll before standup

## Metrics to Track

- **Comments per post** — best signal of virality
- **Follower growth per week** — directional success metric
- **Profile visits from post** — measures curiosity conversion
- **DMs about insurance** — bottom-of-funnel signal

---

## Voice Rules (non-negotiable)

- Write like a smart friend, not a broker
- English only — even when referencing German terms (explain them)
- No hashtag walls (max 3, always at the end)
- No corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "holistic")
- First person always — "I noticed", "I learned", "What surprised me"
- Short sentences. One idea per line. Lots of white space.
