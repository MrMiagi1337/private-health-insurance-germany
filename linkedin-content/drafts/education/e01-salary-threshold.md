## Post

There's a salary threshold in Germany that most expats working in tech are above — and don't know about.

It's called the Jahresarbeitsentgeltgrenze (JAEG). Currently €77,400/year (2026).

Cross it, and you get a choice that most countries don't give their workers: public or private health insurance.

Below it: you're in GKV (public). No choice. ~17% of salary (the 14.6% general rate plus the average supplementary contribution), split with your employer.

Above it: you can stay in GKV — or switch to PKV (private). Your premium is no longer a percentage of your salary. It's a fixed rate based on your age and health at sign-up.

For a 30-year-old software engineer in good health, a solid PKV policy often costs €300-450/month.
GKV contribution at €90k salary: ~€550/month (employee share only).

That's a material difference — before you factor in faster specialist access, better diagnostic coverage, and no co-insurance for non-working spouses.

The decision has long-term implications. PKV works out well if you stay healthy and stay employed. It requires more thinking than just signing the GKV form HR puts in front of you.

But most engineers who qualify don't even know they have a choice.

Did you know about the PKV option when you started working in Germany?
