## Post

Most people think private health insurance premiums only go up.

In Germany, that's not the whole story.

PKV (private health insurance) premiums do increase over time — this is real and you should model it. But there are three structural mechanisms that work in the other direction:

**1. The Altersrückstellung (old-age provision)**
Every PKV policy builds up a capital reserve during your working years. This reserve is specifically designed to offset premium increases as you get older. It's baked into the product by German insurance law.

**2. The statutory 10% surcharge stops at 60**
From age 21 to 60 you pay a mandatory 10% surcharge on top of your premium. It stops at 60, and from 65 the reserve it built up is used to cushion or reduce your premium. Your out-of-pocket costs are designed to ease as you get older, not just climb.

**3. Tariff optimization**
Unlike GKV, you can switch tariffs within your insurer without a new health check. Many policyholders reduce their premium significantly in their 40s and 50s by optimizing their coverage tier — removing services they no longer need.

None of this means PKV is right for everyone. But the "premiums only go up forever" argument misses how the product is actually structured.

A 30-year-old engineer who signs up with a well-rated insurer today, builds their reserve, and actively manages their tariff — can have lower healthcare costs at 60 than someone who stayed in GKV at the same income level.

Counterintuitive. But mathematically solid.
