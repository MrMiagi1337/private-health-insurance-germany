## Post

There's a retirement trap in German public health insurance that almost nobody talks about.

It affects anyone in GKV who doesn't understand rule called KVdR.

Here's how it works:

To qualify for subsidized health insurance as a retiree (KVdR), you need to have been insured in the public system for at least nine-tenths (90%) of the second half of your working life.

If you spent years working abroad, in PKV, or as a freelancer outside GKV, you may not qualify.

What happens then? You're insured voluntarily and pay the contribution yourself, without the share the pension insurance normally covers for KVdR members. On your pension and any other income, that can be around 17%.

For expats who moved to Germany mid-career, this is a real risk.

The engineers I see get hit by this are the ones who assumed "I'll be in GKV for decades, retirement will sort itself out." They didn't model the 90% rule.

None of this is unsolvable. But it requires knowing the rule exists and planning accordingly — ideally in your 30s, not your 50s.

If you're an expat in Germany and you've never heard of KVdR before: that's exactly why this post exists.

Are you tracking the healthcare implications of your long-term plan in Germany?
