## Post

A client told us he waited 6 weeks to see a dermatologist in Germany.

Not because the system is broken. Because nobody explained how it actually works.

In Germany's public health insurance system (GKV), specialist appointments are allocated in a specific order. Urgent cases go first. Routine follow-ups go last. "I noticed something on my skin and I'm a bit worried" lands somewhere in the middle.

6 weeks felt like forever.

What he didn't know: patients with private insurance (PKV) at the same clinic were often seen within the same week. Not because doctors prefer them — but because the billing structure incentivizes faster scheduling.

This isn't a secret. It's just something nobody explains when you sign your first German employment contract.

The public system works. But it works differently depending on which card you show at reception.

We see this pattern constantly. Expats sign up for GKV on autopilot in week one — because HR puts the form in front of them — without realizing they had a choice.
