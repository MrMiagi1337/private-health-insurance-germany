## Post

5 things German software engineers understand about money that most expats take years to learn.

**1. Net salary is the only number that matters.**
German professionals negotiate in net terms, not gross. €70k gross is a completely different conversation than €70k net. Always clarify Netto vs. Brutto before you sign.

**2. The PKV option is a real financial lever — if you qualify.**
Above ~€77,400/year, you're eligible for private health insurance. Many engineers in their 30s use this to get better coverage at lower cost. It's not a loophole. It's a rule most expats never read.

**3. Company pension contributions are negotiable in ways salary often isn't.**
bAV (betriebliche Altersvorsorge) is a tax-efficient way to shift compensation. Reduces your taxable income while building long-term savings. Widely used. Widely ignored by expats.

**4. The Steuererklärung is not optional — it's free money.**
Tax returns in Germany regularly generate refunds, especially with home office deductions, commuting costs, or multiple income sources. Average refund: ~€1,072/year according to official figures (Destatis). Most expats skip it in year one.

**5. Understanding the system is itself a return on investment.**
The German system is complex by design. The professionals who thrive here treat learning it like a side project. The ones who don't pay the difference — often for years.
