## Post

If I was moving to Germany tomorrow, here's exactly how I'd choose between GKV and PKV.

No comparison site. No broker pitch. Just the decision framework I wish I'd had.

**Step 1: Check if you're even eligible for PKV**
You need to earn above €77,400/year (2026), be employed (not a student or certain civil servant track), and pass a basic health check. If you don't qualify, GKV is your lane — and it's a solid system.

**Step 2: Run the math for your actual situation**
→ Single, no kids, healthy, earning €80k+: PKV is almost certainly cheaper and better
→ Family with non-working partner + kids: GKV's free family co-insurance is worth a lot — model it properly
→ Self-employed or freelancer: PKV is often the only realistic option above a certain income

**Step 3: Don't just compare premiums — compare insurers**
Not all PKV policies are equal. The insurer's financial health, their Beitragsanpassungen history (how much they've raised premiums over the years), and the specific tariff structure matter more than the headline premium.

**Step 4: Think long-term**
PKV locks in your health status at sign-up. Signing up at 28 is very different from signing up at 42. If you're eligible now, the clock is ticking.
